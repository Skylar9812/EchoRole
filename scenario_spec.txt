一、职场上下级关系
1. Temporary Extra Task and Boundary Conflict
User A Brief
You are the manager.
At the end of the workday, you suddenly assign an extra task to your employee and ask them to finish it as soon as possible.
In your view, the task is important and the team needs flexibility. You feel that responsible employees should cooperate when urgent work comes up.
You are starting to feel that this employee is not cooperative enough and may care too much about personal boundaries.
User B Brief
You are the employee.
Near the end of the workday, your manager suddenly gives you a new task and expects you to handle it immediately.
You already have other important work to do, and you do not think this new task is truly urgent. You feel that your time and boundaries are not being respected.
You are frustrated because you feel your manager always treats last-minute work as your responsibility.
Turn 1 Current Situation
It is late in the day. The manager has just assigned an extra task, and the employee is clearly unhappy about it.
The manager thinks the employee is being inflexible. The employee thinks the manager is being unreasonable.
Both of you now need to decide how to respond in this conversation.

2. Credit Ownership and Expression Misunderstanding
User A Brief
You are the manager.
A project has just been completed successfully. During a team meeting, you summarize the results and thank the team as a whole, but you do not clearly mention one employee’s specific contribution.
You do not think you did anything wrong. In your view, the project was a team effort, and you were simply giving a general summary.
You are starting to sense that this employee may be unhappy, but you feel they may be overreacting.
User B Brief
You are the employee.
You put a lot of time and effort into this project and solved several key problems. During the team meeting, your manager talked about the success of the project but did not clearly mention your contribution.
You feel disappointed and overlooked. You are beginning to wonder whether your work was ignored or whether someone else is taking the credit.
You are unsure whether to bring it up, because you do not want to seem petty.
Turn 1 Current Situation
The meeting has ended, but the atmosphere feels uncomfortable.
The manager thinks the summary was normal and fair enough. The employee feels that an important contribution was not properly recognized.
Now the two of you have a chance to talk about what happened and what it meant.

3. Unclear Work Standards Leading to Mutual Blame
User A Brief
You are the manager.
You asked your employee to complete a task, but the result does not match what you expected. From your point of view, the employee should have understood what you wanted.
You are annoyed because you feel you already gave enough direction, and now time has been wasted.
You are beginning to think the employee may not be careful or capable enough.
User B Brief
You are the employee.
You completed the task based on the instructions you were given, but your manager is unhappy with the result.
From your point of view, the instructions were vague and the expectations were never explained clearly. You did your best with the information you had.
You feel unfairly blamed for a problem that came from unclear communication.
Turn 1 Current Situation
The task has been completed, but the manager is dissatisfied and the employee feels misunderstood.
The manager thinks the employee failed to execute properly. The employee thinks the manager failed to explain the standard clearly.
Now both of you need to respond to this disagreement.

二、情侣关系
1. Slow Replies and Insecurity
User A Brief
You are in a relationship.
Recently, your partner often replies to your messages very slowly. Sometimes they disappear for a long time without explaining why.
You feel ignored and unimportant. You are starting to wonder whether your partner still takes the relationship seriously.
You do not want to sound overly emotional, but you are clearly bothered by this.
User B Brief
You are in a relationship.
Your partner is upset because you often reply late to messages.
From your point of view, this is mostly because you are busy or not always checking your phone. You do not see it as a sign of losing interest.
You are beginning to feel pressured, because you think your partner is reading too much into normal communication habits.
Turn 1 Current Situation
One person feels hurt and neglected because of slow replies. The other feels unfairly pressured and misunderstood.
The issue is no longer just about messages — it is now about attention, reassurance, and expectations in the relationship.
You now need to talk about what this means and what should change.

2. Forgotten Important Date
User A Brief
You are the girlfriend.
An important date — such as your anniversary or another meaningful day in the relationship — has just passed, and your boyfriend forgot it.
You feel disappointed and hurt. To you, remembering this date is a sign of care, effort, and emotional attention.
You are trying not to overreact, but you feel that this mistake may reflect how seriously he takes the relationship.
User B Brief
You are the boyfriend.
You forgot an important date that your girlfriend cares about.
You did not do it on purpose, and you may feel that dates and formal moments are not the most important part of love. Still, you can see that she is clearly upset.
You are now caught between feeling guilty and feeling that the situation is becoming more serious than you expected.
Turn 1 Current Situation
The important date has already passed, and the disappointment is now clear.
The girlfriend feels emotionally neglected and hurt. The boyfriend knows he made a mistake, but may not fully understand why it matters so much.
Now the two of you need to talk about what this moment means and how to deal with it.

3. Different Boundaries with Opposite-Sex Friends
User A Brief
You are in a relationship.
Recently, you have become uncomfortable with how your partner interacts with a friend of the opposite sex. The communication seems frequent and emotionally close.
You are not necessarily saying your partner has done something wrong, but you feel the boundary is becoming unclear.
You are feeling uneasy, jealous, and uncertain about how to raise the issue without sounding controlling.
User B Brief
You are in a relationship.
Your partner is unhappy about your closeness with a friend of the opposite sex.
From your point of view, the friendship is normal and open. You do not think you have crossed a line.
You feel frustrated because you believe your partner should trust you, and you do not want to be treated as if you did something inappropriate.
Turn 1 Current Situation
A disagreement has started over what counts as an acceptable boundary with someone outside the relationship.
One person feels insecure and uneasy. The other feels distrusted and restricted.
Now you need to talk about trust, boundaries, and what each of you considers appropriate.

4. One Wants to Talk, One Wants to Avoid
User A Brief
You are in a relationship.
After a recent conflict, you want to talk about it as soon as possible. You believe problems should be discussed directly and clearly.
When your partner stays silent or asks for space, you feel anxious and abandoned. To you, avoiding the conversation makes the problem worse.
You are pushing for communication because you want clarity and emotional security.
User B Brief
You are in a relationship.
After a recent conflict, you do not want to talk immediately. You need time to calm down and sort out your thoughts first.
When your partner keeps pushing to talk right away, you feel pressured and emotionally cornered. To you, this makes communication harder, not easier.
You are trying to protect yourself from escalation, but your partner sees it as avoidance.
Turn 1 Current Situation
A conflict has already happened, and now the two of you disagree on how to handle the aftermath.
One person wants immediate discussion. The other wants distance and time.
Now you need to decide how to respond to each other without making the conflict worse.

三、亲子关系
1. Disagreement About Major / Career Direction
User A Brief
You are the parent.
Your child is choosing a future major and career direction. They want to study something in the humanities, such as linguistics or a foreign language.
You strongly hope they will study medicine and become a doctor in the future. In your view, medicine is more stable, respected, and practical.
You believe you are thinking about your child’s long-term security, even if they do not understand that right now.
User B Brief
You are the child.
You want to study a humanities-related subject, such as linguistics or a foreign language, because you are genuinely interested in it and can imagine building a future around it.
But your parents want you to study medicine and become a doctor. You feel they are focusing only on stability and social status, not on who you are or what you want.
You are frustrated because you feel your future is being decided for you.
Turn 1 Current Situation
A serious disagreement has developed around your future path.
The parent believes they are protecting the child’s future. The child believes their own interests and identity are not being respected.
Now the two of you need to talk about what matters more: stability, personal fit, or something in between.

2. Conflict Over Phone / Game Time
User A Brief
You are the parent.
You feel your child spends too much time on their phone or playing games. You are worried it is affecting study, sleep, and daily habits.
You believe you have to step in before things get worse. From your point of view, this is about responsibility and discipline.
You are becoming stricter because you feel your child is losing control.
User B Brief
You are the child.
Your parent keeps criticizing how much time you spend on your phone or on games.
From your point of view, this is one of the few ways you can relax and have some personal space. You do not think your parent fully understands your stress or your daily life.
You feel controlled and judged, rather than understood.
Turn 1 Current Situation
The topic of phone or game use has become a repeated source of tension at home.
The parent sees a problem of discipline and control. The child sees a problem of pressure and lack of understanding.
Now both of you need to respond to the growing frustration.

3. Hurt from Public Comparison by Parents
User A Brief
You are the parent.
You sometimes compare your child to other young people — such as classmates, relatives, or “other people’s children” — because you hope it will motivate them to improve.
You do not think of it as an attack. In your view, comparison is a practical way to push your child to work harder and take things more seriously.
You may not realize how deeply your child is affected by these comments.
User B Brief
You are the child.
Your parent often compares you to other people in front of relatives, family friends, or even in everyday conversation.
You feel embarrassed, hurt, and never good enough. Instead of motivating you, the comparison makes you feel judged and emotionally distant from your parent.
You are starting to stop sharing your real thoughts because you do not feel safe being vulnerable.
Turn 1 Current Situation
A recent comparison has once again left the child feeling hurt and misunderstood.
The parent thinks they are encouraging improvement. The child experiences it as public criticism and emotional damage.
Now the two of you have to face the meaning of these repeated comparisons.
