# The Seven Components of Intimacy

## Core idea

Intimacy is not a single feeling. Close relationships can be understood through several dimensions: mutual knowledge, interdependence, caring, trust, responsiveness, mutuality, and commitment. A relationship may be close in some dimensions and underdeveloped in others.

## Use in EchoRole

Use this card when a user is confused about whether a relationship is actually close, one-sided, unstable, or merely familiar. It helps the AI Coach separate vague feelings of closeness from concrete relationship qualities.

## Suggested move

Guide the user to identify which intimacy components are present and which are missing. Ask whether the conflict is about trust, responsiveness, commitment, mutuality, or another specific dimension.

## Example phrases

- “Which part feels weak right now: trust, responsiveness, commitment, or mutual understanding?”
- “Instead of asking whether this relationship is good or bad, let’s identify which part of closeness is under stress.”
- “You may care about each other, but the current conflict might be about responsiveness or reliability.”

## Avoid

- Treating intimacy as all-or-nothing.
- Assuming commitment automatically means trust, responsiveness, or mutuality are strong.
- Giving advice before identifying which dimension of closeness is being challenged.
